What am I looking at?

This is a visual essay contextualizing Poem Car, a collection of 15 poems about advertising and chaos. Poem Car was written by Oritsemughone Ogbemi in 2024.

Thinking in Shades of Lipstick, Bamboo Skyscrapers, Anger Album, Frangipani, Good Grief, Car Poem/Swamp Rose, Upset Stomach, Parenting Poem, Erica Poem, Regret Egret, Anthill Poem, Here it Comes, Angel-eyes, Blissful Poem..., Frowning
As things change (though they are always changing), I've been thinking about the relationship between poetry and commerce. When I was in graduate school, I had a brief friendship with someone whom I will call G.
G and I are both poets and essayists.
When we were still getting to know each other, we took a walk, and G told me that her birthday was coming up.
"Nice," I responded.
"Yes. So where's my lamborghini?"
G doesn't remember making that joke, but actually the joke changed everything for me. If I ever see G again, it is the first thing I will think of. Because I laugh whenever I remember it, and I like laughing, I'll be fond of her for the rest of my life.

A beautiful poem can feel like the right purchase or a display of charm. This is about handling power. Some of us believe that the safest way of handling power is to let it go, but that can be dangerous. In many situations, somebody has to take control of something, otherwise matters spiral, and it's better if its somebody who cares about the people involved and the outcome.

The safest ways of handling power, from my perspective, are to wield it for the purposes of surprise, delight, restoration, and caretaking. A poem can do all four of those, in a way. Like a lamborghini for your birthday or a good joke from someone you've just met.

I think we can also get the feeling while buying a gift or being in stimulating relationships with others. Poem Car is about that, as well as the chaos of advertising.

A poem is such a tiny thing. It suggests powerlessness. The main reason I have written about advertising is that it's almost all I see and hear. But the second reason is that against its will, advertising teaches us that poems are not helpless. Language is everywhere.

I am not helpless. You are not helpless... Help yourself to some language.
