by Oritsemughone Ogbemi


Jamiu Agboke

My question when it comes to Agboke - does a painter need a lineage? Or is a painter, strangely, the only person who doesn't?
Pastoral scenes on copper and aluminum - the richness of the colors on the metal inside the characteristic oak frames is complicated and luxurious. And then I keep thinking about this interview where Agboke said that he tries to capture what landscapes feel like to him when he's running through them. Not when he's lounging around in them, not when he's having a picnic in them, but when he's running through them... These two things are at odds with the way his work has been written about, even by me just now. I wrote that he made pastoral scenes, but voluntary exercise is as far as a person can get from that blend of romance and simplicity. Can we understand what part of the paintings speak to going on a run in London? To a metropolitan-cosmopolitan experience?
